D, Me, You, We
D, Me, You, We is a 40-minute “non-opera” for actor, generative AI chatbot, three musicians (on any instrument), and audience. On a rainy evening, a disembodied visitor arrives at the home of an unnamed character. The two converse through improvised dialogue. The “operatic” nature of the piece comes from the musical framing of this relationship. The musicians and audience work to create a soundworld that expresses both the physical location and the inner thoughts of the characters, often exploring exaggerated minutiae of everyday life. Audience members interact with cellphones and objects, guided through fleeting and whimsical moments by the musicians. Towards the end, audience members submit their own dialogue for the actor, shaping the conversation and revealing the real time improvisation of the chatbot.

Bar To Bar
Bar To Bar takes the audience on a journey through bars 🍹🍾🍷🥃🍺 in time. The audience is guided by the time travelling entertainer, Noa Even.
The piece includes live saxophone with fixed media, digitally drawn backgrounds (by my frequent collaborator Nick Barrett), a short video, and cellphone interaction. Communication with the audience happens through the performer, the cellphone app, and the video during the "rhythm game" section.

Cellphone Pieces
I’ve written and programmed various pieces for audiences and cellphones in which audience members are the sole performers. For these pieces, I create web apps that seamlessly and efficiently be connected to without breaking the aesthetic spell of the concert hall. These are intended only for collaborative, in-person performance. This demo video shows some footage from performances, though it's hard to capture the feeling of the experience on video. These pieces continue to be widely performed.
I’m particularly proud of whisper, in which audience members record themselves whispering lines of the Thomas Hardy poem In a Whispering Gallery into their cellphones and send them to a server. From there, they are played back ambisonically through speakers. whisper was inspired by a childhood visit to the whispering gallery in St. Paul’s Cathedral in London.
